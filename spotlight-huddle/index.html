Expand · Bem

Fleetio as the anchor, Carrot to explain expansion

Fleetio is the larger established account. Carrot gives us a more concrete sequence of increasing responsibility. Use them together to explain why better models could make Bem a stronger business.

Expanded passage to work through with Daniel

Bem is interesting because a customer can start with one specific problem and gradually trust it with much more.

Take Fleetio, which makes software for managing vehicle fleets. A mechanic’s invoice arrives, and its contents need to become a usable service record. Bem says 99% of those documents now pass through without human intervention. Fleetio has become its largest paying customer; the fundraising memo lists an annual contract of about $250,000.

That’s already meaningful. But the ambition extends beyond processing documents. Once Bem understands the customer’s information, systems and rules, it can start taking responsibility for the work that follows.

Carrot makes that progression easier to see. It manages fertility benefits. Initially, Bem extracted information from claims and receipts. Its materials describe expanding into preparing claims, checking benefit limits and helping make decisions that a human approves. The annual contract grew from $50,000 to $86,000. The next expansion being discussed is handling the adjudication process end to end.

That’s why better models could make Bem a more valuable business. As the technology becomes capable of handling more steps, Bem can offer to take on more of the customer’s operation. It already has the integration, the business context and a history of corrections from doing the work.

What we’re watching is whether customers keep entrusting Bem with more responsibility—and whether that becomes a larger, lasting commercial relationship.

The contract figures come from the fundraising memo: Fleetio’s listed contracted floor is $251K; the passage rounds it to about $250K. Full Carrot adjudication remains scoped work in the detailed customer description. The $500K expansion opportunity is not a completed contract, and these materials do not establish a $1M contract. The 99% zero-human-touch figure is Bem’s reported Fleetio result. Bem memo ↗ · Data room ↗

The progression I want to describe is from external penetration testing to earning codebase access and becoming part of the deployment cycle. That is increasing responsibility and trust in practice.

Phrase the purchasing point as strong internal teams still choosing a specialist: Ramp also builds its own security agents. Veria’s public materials support continuous code review and identify PostHog as a customer.

The future architecture isn’t settled, and these opportunities can coexist. What connects the founders is choosing a meaningful problem and pursuing it with conviction while the technology changes.
